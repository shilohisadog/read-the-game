Watch the game

Press play and just watch — each play narrated in plain language, goals called with the scorer and assists, the pace easing for the big moments. That’s the base view. When you want to understand why a team is on top, add a metric layer below — control, high-danger chances, goaltending, or why play stopped. Nothing is invented; every layer shows its work.

Add a metric layer:
Trails: Current moment shows only what is happening now. Keep every mark leaves every attempt on the ice, which builds into a shot chart by the third period — good to study, busy to watch.
Situations: Power plays and an empty net are still hockey — but they aren't even hockey. Switch and watch which attempts drop out, and why.
Players: Same shots, same outcomes, same math — only the drawing changes. Tabletop is the rod-hockey player you grew up with.

Each net carries the name of the team defending it. Ends are held fixed here, so each team always attacks the same net. In the arena they switch every period — this is the one thing on the rink we move, and the coordinates are the league’s own.

Tip: click a ⚡ high-danger chance (amber ring) on the ice to see why it qualified — with trails set to keep every mark, earlier ones stay clickable too.
