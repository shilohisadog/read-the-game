Each note comes from one of three places: the league’s record of this game, the NHL rulebook, or how often something happened across three seasons of games we hold. None of them says which team had the puck — the record does not say — or why anyone did anything. “Events in a row” counts the numbered marks on the ice; three in a row in one zone is our own rule for when the puck has stayed there, and its seconds run between recorded events, because nobody records when the puck crossed a blue line. A typical shift is the middle of every shift we hold.
