1. **Name the thing for what it is, THEN why we are showing it.** A Goals-mode stop on the restart face-off after
   a review reads "Face-off after a review · TOR's goal came off", not "Review · …" — the frame is a face-off.
2. **Never relabel what stopped play.** The league files a goal's whistle under the challenge code, but a challenge
   does not stop play — the GOAL did. Copy says "Goal, NSH challenged that TOR was offside prior to the goal".
3. **One real moment, one stop.** The goal happened when the puck crossed the line, so a goal-line stop followed by a
   goal-frame stop showed it twice; they were merged.
4. **"Are we inventing X?"** — he asks this repeatedly. Answer exactly which parts are the league's record, which are
   read from tracking, and which words are ours. Every stop must be a recorded or tracked frame.
5. **Verbose over brief when it discloses more** ("…to have its goal count; the question was interference with the
   goaltender"), and team abbreviations over "home/visiting". Plain English, never hockey speak ("ruled out on the
   ice" rejected; "in the WSH end" not "attacking zone"). Overturning takes conclusive proof → "the review
   determined / concluded".

**Why:** the site's pitch is "nothing invented"; a label that misnames a frame is a correctness defect to him.
**How to apply:** before shipping any label or stop, ask what the frame IS in the league's record and say that first.
