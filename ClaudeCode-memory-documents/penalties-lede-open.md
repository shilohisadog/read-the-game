## ⭐⭐ THE RULING TO CARRY FORWARD — and it retired my own cleverness
Mid-task, Kevin: *"remember though, these are learning cards, not data-driven,
education driven (for what that's worth)."*

I had replaced the hedged second sentence with a categorical one about our
schema — *"The league records what the call was for, and where"* — reasoning that
**a frequency word is a symptom of a sentence aimed at INSTANCES rather than at
the SCHEMA.** Kevin cut the whole sentence instead, and his reason is better:

> **A sentence about what OUR RECORD CONTAINS is a fact about the pipeline, not
> about hockey.** On a card whose one job is to teach a rule it does not belong
> at all. Delete the sentence about US and the hedge leaves with it.

⭐⭐ **THE HEDGE WAS A SYMPTOM OF THE AUDIENCE, NOT OF THE AIM.** So the doctrine
question never had to be answered — *"does 'usually' count as a number?"* is
unanswerable and was the wrong question. **The test for a rules blurb: does this
sentence teach the RULE, or describe OUR DATA?**

⛔ It cost nothing. Offside's honest-limit slot exists because that rule's moment
genuinely cannot be replayed; here it can, and the card already promises the door
in its `at` line. **Symmetry with offside was my only argument**, and symmetry is
not a reason to teach a novice about a schema.

## ⚠️ (1) THE FACTUAL ERROR WAS ONE PENALTY IN FIVE, NOT AN EDGE CASE
Over **600 published games**, 4,347 penalties with a readable restart, read off
the league's own `situationCode` at the next face-off rather than a count we
infer: the offending team is **short 78.3%** (3,405), **EVEN 18.2%** (793) and
**UP a skater 3.4%** (149). Kevin was right and understated it.

## ⚠️⚠️ (2) THE EXCEPTION TOOK THREE DRAFTS, EACH WRONG THE WAY THE LAST ONE WAS
- **Draft 1, "on the same whistle"** — simultaneity is not what offsets. Of
  coincident calls matching on count AND minutes, **534 of 588 (90.8%)** restart
  even; of those that do not, **55 of 385 (14.3%)**. Simultaneity alone: 60.5%.
- **Draft 2, "takes a matching penalty"** — the correct rulebook term and still
  wrong for a novice. Kevin: *"there can be different minors penalized on the
  same play that offset."* **206 of 588 offsetting pairs — 35% — name two
  DIFFERENT infractions.** The rule cares about the TIME, never the foul.
- **Draft 3, Kevin's** — names the two conditions the rule actually has, and
  states the consequence **positively**: *"both teams skate with the same number
  of players"* is truer than "neither is short", because an offset pair can leave
  the sides at **4-on-4** — even, but not five.

⭐ **AN EXCEPTION IS A CLAIM AND NEEDS ITS OWN MEASUREMENT.** Naming an exception
*feels* like humility, so it does not get checked the way the rule it qualifies
does. Drafts 1 and 2 were each wrong about roughly a third of the cases they
covered, inside a sentence written to stop overstating.

*(The 54 of 588 that do not restart even are not counter-examples: 34 had another
penalty already running that period, and the rest are cross-period carryover and
a pulled goalie. The pair offsets; the imbalance is somewhere else.)*

## ⭐⭐ 2026-09-20 — THE DOUBLE MINOR AND THE MISCONDUCT

Kevin, after the preseason feed brought `roughing-double-minor`: *"a novice won't
know what a 'double minor' is unless we explain it… we should say it's a double
minor at occurrence and move on. also, we'll add it to the penalty learning
card."*

**THE SPLIT: the replay NAMES it, the card DEFINES it.** The caption gains a tag
on any four-minute penalty (the shape the short-handed tag already uses — a fact
about THIS event, amber so it belongs to neither club), and the figure's note
gains the missing entry in a list it already had.

⛔ **THE REASON THE PHRASE WAS HIDDEN DID NOT HOLD.** `penalties.js` drops "double
minor" from the NAME because *"the clock beside the name already says 4:00"* — and
the box chip renders `left`, the time REMAINING. It reads 4:00 for one instant;
scrub into the middle and the page says "High-sticking 3:12" with nothing saying
this one was twice as long. **The assessed duration reached no surface at all.**
A reason that expired without being re-examined.

⭐ **TRIGGERED ON THE DURATION, NOT THE KEY SPELLING** — four minutes IS two minors
by rule, and a duration cannot drift when the league invents a descriptor, which
it had just done. Sampled 60 published games / 464 penalties: every four-minute
penalty carried a `-double-minor` key and every such key was four minutes; that is
only **5 cases**, so the agreement is pinned over the fixtures rather than trusted.

⭐⭐ **AND THE MISCONDUCT IS THE EXCEPTION TO THE CARD'S OWN OPENING SENTENCE.** The
blurb says a penalty is *"the offender sits, and his team plays a skater short"*;
a misconduct is the one where the first half is true and the second is not. It is
also the one a reader can SEE and misread: **the penalty box is an OCCUPANCY
display, not a strength display** — `sit` answers strength, the box answers who is
sitting — so a misconduct shows a man in the box while the feed reads 5-on-5.
⚠️ I nearly reported that as a bug; `app.js` already said *"a ten-minute misconduct
puts a man in the box and takes nobody off the ice (23 of 332 penalties
measured)"*. **The disagreement was between the code and the CARD, and it pointed
the opposite way from the one I assumed** ([[verify-inherited-claims]]).

⛔ **NO FREQUENCY ON THE CARD.** The rules half says what the record CONTAINS; only
the measurements half says how often — and an existing check enforces it hard: *a
note may carry no digit at all*, which is why "four" and "ten" are spelled as
words.

⭐ **THE CHECK WORTH REUSING: a term the replay puts on screen must be NAMED AND
PRICED on the card that teaches it.** Without it, someone edits the card copy,
the tag becomes the unexplained jargon the change was made to remove, and nothing
goes red. Proven by renaming the term on the card.

**Measured while deciding:** four-minute penalties are **5 of 464** sampled, and
`high-sticking` is the ONLY displayed word carrying two durations (43 at two
minutes, 5 at four). Misconducts are **9 of 115** across the fixtures. The
vocabulary arrivals that started it are in [[season-restart-traps]].
